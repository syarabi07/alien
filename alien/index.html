The Question
01

Are aliens real? Are we alone? Everyone assumes Islam is silent on the subject — that extraterrestrial life is purely a Western, scientific preoccupation. But the answer is not hidden in some obscure scholarly text. It is literally in Surah Al-Fatiha — the first chapter, the one you recite seventeen times a day.

A quick fact, so you feel the scale of this: the observable universe contains roughly two trillion galaxies. Each one holds billions of stars, each star potentially hosts planets; and the whole expanse is expanding constantly — and Allah created all of it. Do you genuinely think all of that was merely decoration?

Rabb Al-ʿĀlamīn
02

Alḥamdulillāhi rabb il-ʿālamīn. You have said this your whole life: "Lord of the Worlds" — worlds, plural, multiple. But here is where Arabic becomes extraordinary. The word al-ʿālamīn is not a random plural. In Arabic grammar, the sound masculine plural is reserved for intelligent, rational beings — not rocks, not empty space, but beings that think and know. When Allah calls Himself Rabb al-ʿālamīn, He is declaring Himself the Lord of multiple realms filled with conscious creation. You have been reciting this your entire life.

The Verse That Ends the Debate
03
Surah Ash-Shūrā · 42:29

And of His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth and what He has dispersed throughout them of creatures. And He, for gathering them when He wills, is competent.

Read that again: whatever living creatures He has dispersed throughout them — "them" referring to the heavens and the earth, not earth alone. Allah is telling us He scattered living beings across the cosmos. This is not interpretive gymnastics; this is the Quran in plain text.

The Arabic word used here is dābba. It does not mean angels; it does not mean jinn. Its root means to walk, to crawl, to move along a surface — something biological, physical, material. And Surah An-Nūr confirms this: Allah says He created every dābba from water. We know angels are made from light and jinn from smokeless fire — but dābba? Water-based creatures, living and breathing, scattered across the heavens? The Quran told us to "follow the water" long before NASA made it their entire brand identity.

Not Angels — Something Else
04
Surah An-Naḥl · 16:49

And to Allah prostrates whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth of creatures, and the angels as well.

Notice how Allah separates dābba and the angels. The Arabic conjunction wa distinguishes two different categories. So whatever these creatures are in the heavens, they are not angels. They are something else — something physical, something that walks, something that submits to Allah in its own way. Somewhere out there, an alien is in sujūd right now. That is not a conjecture; that is the Quran.

Ibn ʿAbbās & the Seven Earths
05

Then there is the narration from Ibn ʿAbbās — the Prophet's cousin and one of the greatest scholars of Quranic interpretation. When asked about the verse "seven heavens and of the earth, alike thereof," he said: "There are seven Earths, and in every Earth there is a prophet like your prophet, an Adam like your Adam, and a Noah like your Noah."

Al-Ḥākim recorded this and authenticated the chain of transmission; Al-Dhahabī agreed. Some scholars considered it unusual, but being unusual does not make something false — and Ibn ʿAbbās was not known for fabrication.

What Did the Angels Already Know?
06

In Surah Al-Baqarah, when Allah tells the angels He is placing a caliph on Earth, they respond: "Will You place therein one who spreads corruption and sheds blood?" But how did the angels know humanity would shed blood? Adam had not yet been created; no human had done anything yet. Unless they had seen this before. Unless there had been others — in other worlds, rising and falling across the cosmos.

The universe is 13.8 billion years old. Earth is 4.5 billion. Homo sapiens have existed for roughly 300,000 years. In the grand timeline of existence, we are the final episode of an extraordinarily long series.

Al-Khallāq — The Continuous Creator
07

The Quran calls Allah al-Khallāq — not merely "the Creator," but the continuous Creator; the One who is always creating, always has been, and always will be. Do you genuinely think that across 13.8 billion years, spanning billions of galaxies and trillions of planets, Allah created life only once — on this one rock?

The question was never "Does Islam allow for aliens?" The real question is: why did we forget what we already knew?

✦   ✦   ✦

The next time you stand in salāh and recite Al-Fātiḥa, remember — you are not simply acknowledging the Lord of Earth. You are acknowledging the Lord of civilizations you have never met, creatures you have never seen, and worlds you will never visit.

And somehow, in this incomprehensibly vast universe, He still hears your duʿāʾ, still counts your sajda, and still calls you His creation. That is not insignificance — that is the ultimate privilege.

If the Quran has been hinting at this for over 1,400 years — what else have we been reciting without truly understanding?
